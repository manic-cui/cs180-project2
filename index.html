Part 1.1: Convolutions from scratch

Slide the flipped kernel on the image and sum the products. Pad zeros so output has same size ("same" mode), kh//2 rows on top and kh-1-kh//2 at bottom, same as scipy, so even size kernel like Dx also match.

Four loops version:

def conv_4loops(im, k):
    H,W=im.shape
    kh,kw=k.shape
    ph=kh//2
    pw=kw//2
    kf=k[::-1,::-1]      # flip kernel, this is convolution not correlation
    # pad zero, output same size
    padded=np.zeros((H+kh-1, W+kw-1))
    padded[ph:ph+H, pw:pw+W]=im
    out=np.zeros((H,W))
    for i in range(H):
        for j in range(W):
            s=0.0
            for u in range(kh):
                for v in range(kw):
                    s=s+padded[i+u, j+v]*kf[u,v]
            out[i,j]=s
    return out

Two loops, inner two loops become numpy:

def conv_2loops(im, k):
    H,W=im.shape
    kh,kw=k.shape
    ph=kh//2
    pw=kw//2
    kf=k[::-1,::-1]
    padded=np.zeros((H+kh-1, W+kw-1))
    padded[ph:ph+H, pw:pw+W]=im
    out=np.zeros((H,W))
    for i in range(H):
        for j in range(W):
            # inner two loop change to numpy
            out[i,j]=np.sum(padded[i:i+kh, j:j+kw]*kf)
    return out

Runtime. Results are same, only float error. Python loop is about 200 times slower than scipy for the 9x9 box. Two loops version call numpy once per pixel, so for tiny Dx it is even slower than four loops.

Boundary. Zero padding treat outside as black, so box filter get a dark frame and derivative filter get fake edge on the border. scipy can mirror the image instead (boundary='symm'), I use that in 1.2 and 1.3.

Part 1.2: Finite difference operator

Convolve cameraman (float in [0, 1]) with Dx, Dy, then gradient magnitude:

|∇I| = sqrt( (∂I/∂x)² + (∂I/∂y)² )

Edge image = magnitude bigger than a threshold.

I choose 0.25. Below 0.2 the grass become many white dots. At 0.3 the buildings on the right and the far tripod leg start to disappear. 0.25 keep the coat, tripod and most of skyline, only few grass dots left. Remove all grass dots also remove the weak real edges, so it is a trade off.

Part 1.3: Derivative of Gaussian filter

2D Gaussian = cv2.getGaussianKernel(11, 1.5) outer product itself. Blur first, then same as 1.2.

Difference: gradient values are much smaller, threshold go down from 0.25 to 0.06. Edges become thicker and continuous, grass texture almost gone because blur remove it before derivative. Less noise and less missing edge at same time.

DoG: one convolution instead of two

(I * G) * Dx = I * (G * Dx), so convolve Gaussian with Dx, Dy first, image only convolve once.

Verify: two ways differ at most 1.1e-15, edge maps exactly same.

Part 2.1: Image sharpening (unsharp masking)

Blur = low frequency, original − blur = high frequency. Add α times high frequency back:

sharp = I + α (I − G*I) = ((1+α) δ − α G) * I

Right side is one convolution, the unsharp mask filter. Two ways give same image (diff 6e-14).

Taj Mahal, σ = 1.5

α = 0.5 to 1 the carving become more clear. α = 2 halo appear around dark trees. α = 4 the sky become grainy, JPEG noise is also high frequency. Sharpening only make existing edges stronger, no new information.

Doe Library, σ = 2

Columns stand out at α = 1, α = 2 a bit too strong. Flat sky stay flat, no high frequency to add.

Evaluation: blur a sharp image and sharpen it again

Sharp lion photo, blur σ = 2, then unsharp mask with same σ.

It looks more clear again, but the thin hairs of mane do not come back, blur already removed them. α = 5 only add halo. Sharpen is not the inverse of blur.

Part 2.2: Hybrid images

Hybrid = low frequency of one picture + high frequency of another. Close see high frequency, far see low frequency. Low pass: Gaussian σlow. High pass: image minus Gaussian σhigh. All gray.

Alignment is the starter code with eye positions typed by hand: recenter, rescale, rotate, crop. The low frequency picture is the rotated one, black corner is less obvious after blur. Black borders come from this padding and rotation.

Part 2.3: Gaussian and Laplacian stacks

Stack is pyramid without downsample. Gaussian stack: every level is last level blurred, σ doubled (2, 4, 8, 16, 32). Laplacian stack: neighbour levels subtract, last level is the most blurry one, so sum of all is the original (error 1e-16). No built-in pyramid function used.

Part 2.4: Multiresolution blending

Laplacian stacks of A and B, Gaussian stack of the mask, blend every level then add up:

Lout,i = GM,i · LA,i + (1 − GM,i) · LB,i , out = Σi Lout,i

Mask is more blurry in higher level, so fine detail cut sharp but colour mix in a wide band, seam is hidden. 6 levels, σ = 2 doubling, colour channel by channel.

The most important thing I learned

The most direct feeling is how an image separate to low and high frequency: blur is the low part, only shape and colour; original minus blur is the high part, only edges and texture. Add high part back is sharpen, high part of one image on low part of another is hybrid, split to many bands and blend each is multiresolution blending. I also become more familiar with treating image as signal (convolution, Gaussian, gradient, Fourier) and the common image operations like blur, sharpen, edge detection, alignment and blending.
